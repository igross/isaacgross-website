How words are counted

Source: the RBA’s official minutes archive. The full body begins at the first substantive section after the attendance lists and ends before “Back to top”. It includes section headings, decisions, substantive additional agenda items and endnotes. It excludes the page title, meeting location/date, participant lists, navigation and footers.

A word is a whitespace-separated token containing at least one letter or number. Hyphenated terms count as one word; punctuation-only tokens and footnote reference numbers do not count. Link labels are included once. Counts are calculated from the complete extracted web text; different word processors may use slightly different conventions.

The second view counts the “Considerations for monetary policy” and “The decision” sections only. This helps distinguish longer policy deliberations from additional agenda items. Text length is not a measure of meeting duration, transparency or policy quality. These are dated observations, not an automatically updating feed.
