How it works

Choose a cash-rate path, apply MARTIN’s responses to the SMP forecast, and minimize the loss over the ten forecast quarters.

Two ways to optimize

Quarter-by-quarter path: jointly chooses ten quarterly cash rates to minimize total horizon loss, subject to the rate and move limits. This is a full-horizon plan, not a sequence of short-sighted one-quarter choices.

Simple policy rule: searches for one set of inflation, unemployment, unemployment-momentum and rate-inertia coefficients, following the form in Gross & Leigh (2022). The rule then sets rates using the resulting inflation and unemployment paths. Its rate is capped by the same limits.

Rule coefficients on inflation, unemployment and momentum are constrained to 0–5; inertia to 0–1. A deterministic search from multiple starting points returns the best rule found, without a global-optimum guarantee. A restricted rule can do worse than the baseline. Rule inertia and the smoothing loss weight are separate parameters.

Targets, timing & limits

The loss is the undiscounted sum of squared inflation and unemployment gaps and actual quarterly cash-rate changes. Defaults follow the paper: weights 1, 1 and 0.5. Inflation targets 2.5%; the 2–3% band is shown for context. This target officially applies to headline CPI and is used here for trimmed mean.

The unemployment target is Isaac Gross’s baseline NAIRU estimate, held constant at 4.89%, as on the scenario page. The initial cash rate is the SMP June 2026 anchor, 4.3%, not today’s rate. March and September baseline forecasts are interpolated between published endpoints.

The rule assumes a constant neutral real rate of 1%, which you can change. Its initial two-quarter unemployment momentum uses the June unemployment anchor for the unavailable pre-horizon quarter. This initialization is an assumption, not another observation.

No terminal loss or uncertainty is added. Decisions near December 2028 ignore effects beyond that date. Results are conditional on the selected weights, targets, limits and forecast horizon.

Model & verification

Uses the same August 2026 SMP forecasts and public MARTIN equations as scenario analysis. Local responses are calculated around the saved 2019 model baseline, with the cash-rate equation replaced each quarter and all other equations retained. This removes the original monetary-policy feedback when setting a new rate path.

Responses are added to the SMP baseline; the model is not re-estimated at the 2026 forecast. This is an independent linear approximation, not an RBA forecast or a replication of the paper’s re-estimated model. Large rate departures and changes in economic relationships are outside the approximation’s strongest use.

The animation moves from the baseline to the solved path. The quarterly path reveals one decision at a time, while the policy rule moves the whole curve at once. The quarterly optimum is still solved jointly across the horizon; the reveal is not a sequence of independent one-period optimizations. Intermediate frames are visual transitions, not solver iterations or new policy decisions. Loss is recalculated from the displayed paths in every frame.

The market reference is fetched directly from Isaac Gross’s cash-rate repository whenever the page loads, and checked hourly while open. The repository’s existing refresh writes the feed. The observation date is always shown; a saved copy is labelled if the live fetch fails.

Market points are ASX cash-rate futures-implied monthly averages, positioned at month-end. They can contain risk premia and differ in timing from the older SMP assumptions. The line stops at the last available contract; it is a reference only and does not enter either optimizer. Quarter-end months are included in the table and CSV where available.
