    How the estimate works

    We start with the RBA F6 average for outstanding loans of your purpose and repayment type. We assign that rate to a reference borrower, then add the differences implied by the published regression. Matching loan type avoids treating an investor’s larger discount as a lower mortgage rate.

    Your benchmark = current loan-type average + adjustments relative to the reference profile

    The reference is an assumption, not the measured average borrower. Its characteristics affect the answer. The market average is balance-weighted, covers participating institutions and combines fixed and variable rates. Updating this average changes the starting level; it does not re-estimate the historical relationships.

    Missing details are replaced by the reference values, never by zero. A missing standalone characteristic therefore makes no adjustment relative to the reference. If income or loan amount is missing, its assumed value also enters the loan-to-income calculation. This is a calculation for an assumed profile, not an average over every possible missing value.

    Reference borrower & calibration assumptions

    The loan type always matches yours. All other reference characteristics can be changed below to see how the calibration affects your result.

    Initial reference: $600,000 loan, $150,000 annual income, 80% original LVR and 24 months old; existing house purchase, no mortgage insurance, direct to lender, full documentation, metro location and PAYG employment. These are site assumptions.

    The research & published coefficients

    Bergmann and Tran, The Distribution of Mortgage Rates (RBA Bulletin, March 2018), Table 1. The regression models discounts from standard variable rates using major-bank securitised loans originated from 2014 and outstanding in December 2017. Its R² is 0.28.

    Positive coefficients mean larger discounts. The calculation reverses that sign for rate adjustments. The intercept and loan-type term cancel against the matched reference. Bank and state coefficients were not published; differences in those effects cannot be included. This tool is independent of the RBA.

    Limits, dates & interpretation

    The historical coefficients describe associations, not the causal effect of changing a characteristic. Pricing practices may have changed. Missing fixed effects, current borrower distributions and prediction-error estimates prevent an exact current conditional mean, confidence interval or percentile ranking.

    Loan age is capped at 47 months, reflecting the historical origination window. The calculator accepts loans up to $1.65 million, where the published loan-size curve turns; this is a conservative calculator limit, not a published sample boundary. Dollar inputs are nominal, with no inflation rescaling. Other unusual combinations may also be outside the historical data’s support.

    Rates refresh when the source data is rebuilt, not automatically in your browser. Your entries remain on this page and are not saved or transmitted. Use the result as context for comparing offers, taking fees, features and refinancing costs into account.
